## Scores aren't probabilities

The last layer of a digit classifier is a matrix-vector product that produces **10 numbers**, one per digit. They're called <Term t="logit">logits</Term>, and they can be anything: $(2.1,\ -0.4,\ 7.3,\ \dots)$. Bigger means "more likely", but they aren't probabilities. They can be negative, and they don't add up to 1.

You need a function that turns any vector of scores into a probability distribution, where:

1. every output is positive,
2. the outputs sum to 1,
3. a bigger score always gets a bigger probability.

Dividing each score by the total fails rule 1 as soon as a score is negative. The fix is to make everything positive first, with the exponential function, and *then* divide by the total.

## Softmax

$$
\mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{z})_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}}
$$

$e^{z}$ is always positive, dividing by the total makes the outputs sum to 1, and $e^z$ grows with $z$, so the order of the scores is preserved. That's the <Term>softmax</Term>. Play with it:

## Three properties worth knowing

**Adding a constant changes nothing.** Add $c$ to every score and every $e^{z_i}$ gets multiplied by $e^c$, top and bottom, so it cancels:

$$
\frac{e^{z_i + c}}{\sum_j e^{z_j + c}} = \frac{e^{c}\,e^{z_i}}{e^{c}\sum_j e^{z_j}} = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}}
$$

Only the *differences* between scores matter. Try the "Add 1 to every score" button above.

**It's a soft version of "pick the biggest".** Dividing the scores by a *temperature* $T$ before the softmax controls how decisive it is. As $T \to 0$, all the probability piles onto the largest score: the output becomes a one-hot vector picking the maximum. That's where the name comes from. As $T$ grows, the distribution flattens towards uniform.

**With two classes, it's the sigmoid.** $\mathrm{softmax}(z, 0)_1 = \dfrac{e^z}{e^z + 1} = \dfrac{1}{1 + e^{-z}} = \sigma(z)$. The sigmoid from Module 2 is the two-class special case.

## Doing it without overflowing

Mathematically, softmax is happy with scores like $(1000, 1001, 1002)$. Computationally, $e^{1000}$ is far beyond the largest 64-bit float ($\approx 1.8 \times 10^{308}$), so it becomes infinity, and $\infty / \infty$ is NaN ("not a number"). A network whose logits drift large would suddenly output garbage.

The fix uses the first property: subtract the **largest** score from every score before exponentiating. The answer doesn't change, but now the biggest exponent is $e^0 = 1$ and nothing can overflow:

$$
\mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{z})_i = \frac{e^{z_i - m}}{\sum_j e^{z_j - m}}, \qquad m = \max_j z_j
$$

Very negative scores still underflow to $e^{-\text{huge}} = 0$, but that's fine: their true probability really is 0 to many decimal places.

## From probabilities to a prediction

To turn the probabilities into a single answer, pick the class with the highest probability: the **argmax**. Since softmax preserves order, that's also the index of the highest *score*. You don't even need the softmax to make a prediction, only to train.

<KeyIdea>

$\mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{z})_i = e^{z_i} / \sum_j e^{z_j}$ turns any scores into probabilities. It only depends on differences between scores, so subtract the maximum first to avoid overflow. The prediction is the argmax.

</KeyIdea>
