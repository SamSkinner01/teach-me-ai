## Two knobs

A convolution layer has two more settings besides the kernel size.

**Padding** adds a border of zeros around the input before sliding the kernel. Without it, every convolution shrinks the image (28 → 26 → 24 …), and the pixels at the edges are covered by fewer kernel positions than those in the middle. With $p = (k - 1)/2$ (1 for a 3×3 kernel), the output is the **same size** as the input, which is why that's often called "same" padding.

**Stride** is how far the kernel jumps between positions. Stride 1 visits every position; stride 2 skips every other one, roughly **halving** the output in each direction. That's a cheap way to shrink feature maps as you go deeper.

## The output-size formula

Along one axis of length $n$, with kernel size $k$, stride $s$ and padding $p$:

$$
n_{\text{out}} = \left\lfloor \frac{n + 2p - k}{s} \right\rfloor + 1
$$

The padded length is $n + 2p$. The kernel's first position is 0 and its last must leave room for all $k$ cells, which gives $\lfloor (n + 2p - k)/s \rfloor$ jumps after the first. When the division isn't exact, the leftover rows and columns at the end are simply never covered.

Getting this right matters: the dense layer after the convolutions needs to know exactly how many numbers come out of them.

<KeyIdea>

Padding adds zeros around the input (and $p = (k-1)/2$ keeps its size); stride sets how far the kernel jumps (stride 2 roughly halves it). The output size is $\lfloor (n + 2p - k)/s \rfloor + 1$.

</KeyIdea>
