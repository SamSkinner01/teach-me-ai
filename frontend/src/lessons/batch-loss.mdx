## A loss for the whole batch

Your network now produces a matrix of logits, one row per example. The loss for a batch is the **average** cross-entropy over its rows:

$$
L = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \text{CE}(\text{logits}_i,\ y_i)
$$

Averaging rather than summing means the size of the gradient doesn't depend on the batch size, so the same learning rate works whether you use batches of 16 or 256.

<NumericCheck
  id="batch-loss-mean"
  question="A batch of two examples has individual losses 0.1 and 0.5. What is the batch loss?"
  answer={0.3}
  tolerance={1e-6}
  explain="The average: $(0.1 + 0.5)/2 = 0.3$."
/>

## Where backpropagation starts

Backprop needs $\partial L/\partial \text{logits}$, a matrix the same shape as the logits. Each example's logits only affect its own term of the average, and from Module 3 you know that term's gradient is $\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{y}$. The $\frac1N$ comes along for the ride:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial\,\text{logits}_i} = \frac{1}{N}\big(\mathrm{softmax}(\text{logits}_i) - \text{onehot}(y_i)\big)
$$

That matrix is the starting point of every backward pass in a classifier. From here on, the chain rule carries it back through each layer.

## Loss vs accuracy

What you actually care about is usually **accuracy**: the fraction of examples where the most likely class is the right one. So why not train on accuracy directly?

Because accuracy is a step function of the weights. Nudge a weight slightly and the predictions almost never change, so the accuracy doesn't change either, and its gradient is zero almost everywhere. Gradient descent would have nothing to follow. Cross-entropy is a smooth stand-in: it keeps rewarding the model for making the right class *more* probable, even before that changes the prediction.

So you train on the loss, but you **report** accuracy: it's what tells you whether the model is any good.

<KeyIdea>

The batch loss is the mean cross-entropy. Its gradient with respect to the logits, $(\text{softmax} - \text{onehot})/N$ row by row, is where backpropagation starts. Accuracy is what you report, but it can't be trained on directly because its gradient is zero almost everywhere.

</KeyIdea>
