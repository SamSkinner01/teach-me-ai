## Entry by entry

Not every operation on matrices is about rows and columns. Many are <Term t="element-wise">element-wise</Term>: they combine two tensors of the **same shape** one matching entry at a time. Matrix addition is one:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 10 & 20 \\ 30 & 40 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 11 & 22 \\ 33 & 44 \end{bmatrix}
$$

So is the **element-wise product**, also called the <Term t="hadamard product">Hadamard product</Term> and written $\odot$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix} \odot \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 12 \\ 21 & 32 \end{bmatrix}
$$

Don't mix it up with matrix multiplication, which gave $\begin{bmatrix} 19 & 22 \\ 43 & 50 \end{bmatrix}$ for the same two matrices. The Hadamard product turns up all over backpropagation: when a gradient passes backwards through an activation function, it gets multiplied, entry by entry, by that function's derivative.

## Applying a function to every entry

You can also apply an ordinary one-number function to every entry. Neural networks do this after almost every layer. The most popular choice is the ReLU, $\text{relu}(x) = \max(0, x)$, which zeroes out the negative entries:

$$
\text{relu}\left(\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 2 \\ 0.5 & -3 \end{bmatrix}\right) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 0.5 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
$$

That's what turned the raw convolution outputs into the feature maps you saw in the welcome demo. You'll meet ReLU and its relatives properly in Module 5.

## Broadcasting

A layer computes $\mathbf{z} = W\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}$ for one input. For a batch of inputs stacked as rows, $Z = XW^\mathsf{T}$ is a matrix with one row per input, but there's still just *one* bias vector $\mathbf{b}$. It should be added to **every row**.

<Term>Broadcasting</Term> is the rule that makes this work: when shapes don't match, a smaller tensor is **stretched** along the missing axis, as if it were copied, though no real copy is needed.

<BroadcastViz />

The usual broadcasting rule compares shapes starting from the **last** axis. Two sizes are compatible if they're equal, or if one of them is 1 (or missing), in which case it gets stretched. So a `[4, 3]` matrix plus a `[3]` vector works: the vector is stretched to `[4, 3]`. But `[4, 3]` plus `[4]` doesn't, because the last sizes are 3 and 4.

<KeyIdea>

Element-wise operations combine matching entries of same-shaped tensors; $\odot$ is *not* matrix multiplication. Broadcasting stretches a smaller tensor along missing axes, like adding one bias vector to every row of a batch.

</KeyIdea>
