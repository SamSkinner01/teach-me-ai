If you remember one operation from this course, make it this one. Every neuron in every neural network computes a dot product.

## The definition

The <Term>dot product</Term> of two vectors of the same length multiplies matching components and adds up the results:

$$
\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = a_1 b_1 + a_2 b_2 + \dots + a_n b_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i b_i
$$

$$
(1,\ 2,\ 3) \cdot (4,\ 5,\ 6) = 1\cdot4 + 2\cdot5 + 3\cdot6 = 32
$$

Notice the result is a single number, a scalar, not a vector.

## What it measures

The arithmetic is simple. What makes the dot product so useful is what it *means*. It turns out that

$$
\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \|\mathbf{a}\|\,\|\mathbf{b}\|\cos\theta
$$

where $\|\mathbf{a}\|$ and $\|\mathbf{b}\|$ are the vectors' lengths and $\theta$ is the angle between them. Since $\cos\theta$ is $1$ when the vectors point the same way, $0$ when they're perpendicular and $-1$ when they point in opposite directions, the dot product measures **how much two vectors agree**:

- **positive**: they point roughly the same way
- **zero**: they're perpendicular, and have nothing in common
- **negative**: they point roughly opposite ways

Drag the arrows and watch the sign. The purple bar is the *projection* of $\mathbf{b}$ onto $\mathbf{a}$: the shadow $\mathbf{b}$ casts on the line through $\mathbf{a}$.

## Length

The length of a vector, called its <Term>norm</Term>, comes from Pythagoras. In 2D, the arrow to $(3, 4)$ is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with sides $3$ and $4$, so its length is $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5$. The same formula works in any dimension:

$$
\|\mathbf{v}\| = \sqrt{v_1^2 + v_2^2 + \dots + v_n^2}
$$

Look closely at what's under the square root: it's $\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{v}$. So

$$
\|\mathbf{v}\| = \sqrt{\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}}
$$

which means once you've written `dot`, you get `norm` almost for free.

## Why neurons use dot products

A <Term>neuron</Term> takes an input vector $\mathbf{x}$ and computes

$$
z = \mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{x} + b
$$

where $\mathbf{w}$ is its weight vector and $b$ its <Term>bias</Term>. Read that through the lens of *agreement*: the neuron's output is large when the input points the same way as its weights. The weights are a **pattern**, and the dot product measures **how strongly the input matches it**.

For a digit classifier, you could imagine the "is this a 1?" neuron having weights shaped like a vertical stroke: positive down the middle of the image, negative at the sides. An image of a 1 has bright pixels exactly where the weights are positive, so the dot product is big. Real networks learn subtler patterns, but the principle is the same.

<KeyIdea>

$\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = \sum_i a_i b_i = \|\mathbf{a}\|\,\|\mathbf{b}\|\cos\theta$: one number measuring how much two vectors point the same way. A vector's length is $\sqrt{\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}}$.

</KeyIdea>
